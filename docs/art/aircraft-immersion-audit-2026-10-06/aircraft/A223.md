## Biggest mismatches

**A220 wingtip identity needs correction.** `scripts/generate-air-014-a220-300.py:397–407` constructs a raked tip “not a winglet”. The newer front/overview fixture shows gently upturned extensions. The exact-variant Airbus photo linked from [R3], visually inspected in temporary storage, shows a distinct swept, canted winglet rising sharply above the wing plane. This is stronger evidence than the source comment alone. It affects exterior silhouette and passenger wing glances. Runtime kit: `game/Airside/Assets/Airside/Art/Models/Aircraft/mdl_a220_300_v01.gltf` and matching bin/FBX.

**The deck has the right broad family signals but generic architecture.** A223 explicitly has sidesticks, five display surfaces and no stowed A320 pilot trays (`P/JetCockpitProfile.cs:14–27`; `P/JetCockpitInterior.cs:62–69,179–187`). Those are useful foundations. However, its four equal upper screens plus small lower screen share the 787 construction branch; pedestal and overhead are common recipes (`:250–288`). The historical fixture visibly shows block consoles, repeated compass graphics and an evenly partitioned overhead. Airbus confirms a separately conceived A220 cockpit with large screens, fewer controls and sidesticks [R4]; being an Airbus sidestick aircraft does not justify an A320 control-panel recipe. Exact display dimensions and switch positions were not verified from an accessible detailed primary photograph, so this audit does not certify them.

**The distinctive 2+3 cabin is encoded but barely communicated.** Source builds `{2,3}` correctly and places its aisle off-centre via the group traversal (`P/PassengerCabinInterior.cs:49–68`). Yet bins and lights are mirrored about the fuselage centre (`:43–47`); the initial outward window view hides that asymmetry. Five rows are capped by nearby full bulkheads, with flat ceiling/walls and rectangular bins (`:38–46`). The historical windows visibly have faceted oval rims and dark rectangular corner gaps. Aperture constants are shared by every aircraft: 0.32 × 0.44 m (`:94`), while Airbus publishes 11 × 16 inches for A220 windows [R2]. Treat that as an approximate visual anchor, not certified inner-trim dimensions. Interior width also needs intentional fitting: source uses 3.346 m between window planes; Airbus states 3.28 m maximum cabin width [R3]. These are different datums, not proof that a simple subtraction is the fix.
