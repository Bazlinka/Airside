## Biggest mismatches

**The window and wall system loses aircraft identity.** `PassengerCabinInterior.cs:94` gives every aircraft a 0.32 × 0.44 m ellipse, rather than a type-defined aperture. More significantly, `:47` repeats windows at seat pitch, 0.79 m for A359, while `scripts/generate-air-009-a350-900.py:107–120` authors exterior stations at 0.508 m. Project dimensions are not certified measurements. The fuselage is suppressed in interior mode, so agreement cannot be demonstrated just by seeing an unobstructed hole. Seat and window rhythms should be independent.

**Widebody proportions are present but the cabin shape is generic.** A359 profile width is 5.672 m versus Airbus' 5.71 m maximum; other cross-section dimensions remain unverified. The builder uses a flat ceiling at eye +0.99 m, flat vertical sidewalls, continuous box bins, and abrupt bulkheads only about 1.975 m ahead/behind the selected row (`PassengerCabinInterior.cs:38–46,70–72`). Turning inward can reveal a short rectangular room rather than a long sculpted twin-aisle cabin. Nine abreast is a valid representative baseline; later Airbus offerings also permit ten abreast, so avoid calling this universal. [Airbus A350-900](https://www.aircraft.airbus.com/en/aircraft/a350/a350-900), [2022 cabin enhancements](https://www.aircraft.airbus.com/en/newsroom/stories/2022-10-a350-introduces-new-performance-and-airspace-cabin-enhancements-for-all).

**A350 differs from A330 already, but its strongest differentiator is simplified incorrectly.** `JetCockpitProfile.cs:28–29` selects A350 versus classic Airbus decks. `JetCockpitInterior.cs:173–177` builds five equal upper screens and a smaller lower screen, all in a flat arrangement. Airbus describes six identical large-format displays with the lateral displays angled inward. Shared pedestal and overhead methods (`:250–287`) retain the same twin small FMS units and regular switch grid across jets. The historical panel shows this generic treatment. [Airbus FAST special A350, printed pages 14–15](https://www.aircraft.airbus.com/sites/g/files/jlcbta126/files/2022-04/FAST_specialA350.pdf).

## First pass and acceptance

First pass: **window station/reveal**, **sculpted twin-aisle cabin**, **six-screen A350 deck geometry**. These address the greatest identity gaps without touching simulation.

Source acceptance: independent seat/window stations, nine seats/two aisles fitting cross-section, consistent screens/lateral angles, hidden hull versus retained wing/engine prefixes (`CockpitExteriorVisibility.cs:12–24`), and unchanged exterior restoration when switching modes. Retain material batching (`PassengerCabinInterior.cs:75–90`) and no per-frame geometry creation.

Future visual acceptance, not performed: packaged cockpit/left/right/exterior switching on A359, forward/side/down/up/inward views, day/dusk/night, wing/horizon visibility in level flight and bank, 35°–85° interior zoom with the 0.035 m near clip, small-window HUD clearance, and measured frame-time comparison. Existing fixture passes do not prove these outcomes.
