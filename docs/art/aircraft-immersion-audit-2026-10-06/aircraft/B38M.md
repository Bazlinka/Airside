## Biggest mismatches

**The cockpit has the correct screen count, but a generic enclosure.** `JetCockpitProfile.cs:15–23` selects four displays, eye Y 5.59, Z −2.65 and half-width 1.15 metres; B738 uses the same Z/width with Y 5.62. These are project fits, not certified pilot-eye dimensions. `JetCockpitInterior.cs:91–131` builds the shared width-driven shell; `:152–159` uses a box glareshield and eight identical square knobs. The historical forward/side angles show broad simple panes and thick framing. Refit around the existing exterior pane bounds before moving the eye merely to reveal more panel.

**The MAX display differentiation stops at four rectangles.** `JetCockpitInterior.cs:181–185` creates four equal landscape panels, then `:197–241` renders simplified horizons/compass graphics. The historic panel view confirms this. Boeing confirms four large 38 cm displays [1]; visual bezel proportions, panel rake and credible graphic hierarchy matter more than adding avionics functions. Source `:189–192` additionally places game telemetry and “LOCAL VIEW” on the physical panel; the shared `FlightViewHud.cs:56–76` already supplies aircraft identity and metrics.

**The passenger shell is a five-row generic box.** `PassengerCabinInterior.cs:38–47` uses flat ceiling, end bulkheads, continuous rectangular bins and windows separated by seat pitch. `:92–114` gives every aircraft the same 0.32 × 0.44 m oval aperture and 45 mm reveal. B38M's `PassengerCabinProfile.cs:21` specifies 3+3 seating and 0.79 m pitch, identical to B738 except window height. Its exterior generator uses 0.508 m window spacing and 0.25 × 0.36 m panes (`scripts/generate-air-005-narrowbody-737-8.py:195–206`). Adjacent interior windows therefore cannot all coincide with exterior stations. These are source dimensions, not manufacturer measurements. The central fitted seat does not validate surrounding apertures.

## Acceptance for subsequent implementation

Source/geometry review should verify distinct four-screen MAX versus six-screen NG layouts; apertures derived from actual B38M window stations; independent seat/window pitch; continuous wall/reveal topology; eye/panel/windscreen clearance; and retained engine, wing and both winglet surfaces during both passenger views. Keep batching and avoid per-frame geometry creation.

Future runtime review must capture forward/left/right/down/overhead cockpit, both passenger sides with inward glances, and exterior switching at day/dusk/night, 35–85° FOV and enabled motion. Check for clipping, artificial end walls, lost nacelles, display glare, HUD obstruction and restoration of exterior renderers. Inspect current packaged source through a full view cycle. None of these runtime checks was performed in this audit.
