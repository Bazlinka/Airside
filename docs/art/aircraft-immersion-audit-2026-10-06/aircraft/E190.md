## Identity and biggest mismatches

The exact original-E190 Embraer APM, Section 2.4/2.5, specifies a representative 2+2 economy cabin, 2.74 m width, 0.49 m aisle and 0.82 m pitch, with a curved cross-section. Operator configurations vary. Its rounded cockpit-edge description supports softening the present block forms. The E190 product page explicitly distinguishes the newer E2 generation. [Embraer APM](https://embraer.com/media/jwsj1u1u/e190apm_apm_190-1.pdf), [E190](https://www.embraer.com/e-jets/e190/en/).

Current source already gets two important identity cues right: five front displays with central EICAS (`JetCockpitInterior.cs:179–185`) and ram horns instead of sidesticks or conventional closed yokes (`:73–84`). Honeywell's original E-Jets upgrade document confirms five forward displays; it is a family reference, not authority for every E190 knob or installed upgrade. An exact manufacturer ram-horn detail photograph was not verified in this audit; retain the existing cue and require that reference before claiming precise grip dimensions. [Honeywell E-Jets](https://aerospace.honeywell.com/content/dam/aerobt/en/documents/learn/platforms/airlines-%26-cargo/brochures/n61-2230-000-000_embraer_ejets.pdf).

The largest cabin limitation is the shared rectangular five-row box. `PassengerCabinInterior.cs:38–47` builds flat ceiling/bulkheads and continuous box bins. The 2+2 arrangement is correct, but `:49–65` derives every seat from available width: E190 yields nominal 0.555 m seat cells and a fixed 0.44 m aisle. This should be reconciled with the selected reference arrangement and model shell rather than passed off as an exact replica. The capture cannot assess the whole cabin because its framing centres on the window.

The close aperture is also disconnected from the exterior treatment: shared interior aperture 0.32 × 0.44 m with 32 segments and shallow reveal (`PassengerCabinInterior.cs:94–114`), versus authored exterior panes 0.23 × 0.33 m (`scripts/generate-air-013-e190.py:162–170`). These are different authored representations, not proven real-aircraft errors. Shared visibility hides the hull while retaining wings/engines (`CockpitInterior.cs:25–46`), making coherent local sidewall depth especially important.

## First pass and acceptance

Start with the curved 2+2 shell, integrated window surround and rounded ram horns. They address the largest close-view forms without avionics scope or a full aircraft rebuild.

Future implementation source checks should establish distinct seat/window spacing, coherent E190 aperture dimensions, five display positions, two ram-horn columns, and correct restoration of hidden exterior renderers. Reconcile shell offsets with the runtime model; do not substitute manufacturer ground heights for model datums blindly.

Future visual acceptance must inspect cockpit forward/panel/overhead/downward glances, both windows looking outward and inward, and return to exterior during taxi, climb and bank at day/dusk/night. Verify no head/trim clipping, visible adjacent cabin context, plausible wing relationships and readable dark surfaces. Shared status HUD (`AirsidePrototype.Cockpit.cs:234–254`) must remain legible without excessive duplicate text. Assess draw batches and frame time only after implementation. None of these runtime checks was performed here.
